How it works

Light is not a tidy queue. Photons of every colour leave the source at random moments and from every part of it, so the beam is a jumble rather than an ordered stream of colours \u2014 that jumble is what makes it white. To pick out what one colour does, put a filter after the source: only that band reaches the gas, and you can see at once whether anything is absorbed.

Energetic is not the same as useful. Hydrogen\u2019s violet, blue and cyan lines carry more energy than its red one, but all four are absorbed just as readily, because all four start from n = 2. The red 656 nm photon is the least energetic of them and it still excites the electron, from n = 2 up to n = 3. Each dark line in the strip is marked with the jump that made it.

Matching, not merely enough. Choose a filter whose band holds none of the element\u2019s lines and nothing is absorbed, however energetic the photons are. A photon is taken whole or not at all, so its energy has to equal a gap exactly.

Why only certain wavelengths. An electron in an atom can only have certain energies. To move up it must take in exactly the gap between two levels, and a photon is taken whole or not at all. So a photon is absorbed only when hf = E2 − E1. One energy means one frequency, and so one wavelength.

Where the light goes. The electron falls back almost at once and gives the photon back out. It does not remember which way the first photon was going, so it emits in any direction. Only the tiny fraction still heading your way reaches your eye, so that wavelength arrives weaker: a dark line. The energy is not destroyed — look at the cloud from the side and you see the same wavelengths as bright lines.

Hydrogen in the Sun. The four hydrogen lines you can see come from atoms that are already in n = 2. Jumps up from n = 1 need ultraviolet photons, so they leave no line you can see.

The ladders. Hydrogen and sodium are drawn with their real energies. For helium, magnesium, calcium and iron the level schemes are more complicated, so only the two levels involved in each line are drawn, with the gap that matters.
