      How it works

      Why the energies are negative. 0 eV is an electron that has only just escaped. A bound electron has less energy than that, so its energy is negative.

      Absorbing. The photon energy must equal the gap between the electron's level and a higher one: hf = E2 − E1. A photon is taken whole or not at all, so any other energy passes through. Above the ionisation energy the electron is freed and the extra becomes kinetic energy.

      Emitting. Each jump down gives off one photon of exactly the gap. Here every allowed jump down is equally likely.

      Sodium. The outer electron only jumps between s and p, or between p and d, so some gaps give no line.

      Extras. The cloud beside the diagram shows where the electron is likely to be; it only moves while a jump is happening. Occasionally hydrogen lands in a metastable state and leaves it by giving off two photons at once.
