Rays information

Every ray diagram is built from these three. Any two of them fix the image; the third is your check.

Parallel ray

A ray arriving at the lens parallel to the principal axis leaves through the principal focus. For a diverging lens, it spreads out as though it came from the principal focus in front.

Centre ray

A ray passing through the optical centre continues straight without changing direction.

Focal ray

A ray passing through the principal focus in front of the lens leaves parallel to the principal axis. For a diverging lens, the ray travels towards the principal focus on the far side and leaves the lens parallel to the principal axis.

Key words

Tap any orange label on the diagram to jump straight to its meaning.

Principal axis

The straight line through the optical centre, at right angles to the lens. Every distance in a ray diagram is measured along it, and the object always stands on it.

Optical centre C

The exact middle of the lens. A ray that goes through it carries straight on without bending, which makes it the easiest of the three rays to draw.

Principal focus F

For a converging lens, the point where rays that arrive parallel to the principal axis are brought together. For a diverging lens, the point they appear to spread out from. Every lens has one on each side, the same distance away.

Focal length f

The distance from the optical centre to the principal focus. A fatter lens bends light more, so it has a shorter focal length. Move the slider and watch the image jump.

2F

Twice the focal length from the lens. It is only a marker, not a property of the lens, but it is the boundary that decides whether the image comes out bigger or smaller than the object.

Real image

The refracted rays genuinely cross, so light really arrives at that point and you can catch the image on a screen. From a single lens, a real image is always inverted.

Virtual image

The rays leave the lens spreading apart, so they never meet. Traced backwards (the dashed lines) they seem to come from a point. Your eye sees an image there, but no screen will catch it, and it is always upright.

Magnification

Image height divided by object height. Bigger than 1 is magnified, smaller than 1 is diminished. It also equals image distance divided by object distance.

Converging and diverging lenses

A converging (convex) lens is thicker in the middle and brings parallel rays together. A diverging (concave) lens is thinner in the middle and spreads them apart, so it only ever makes small, upright, virtual images.

The three standard rays

Parallel ray   Centre ray   Focal ray
They all start at the tip of the object. Where they meet after the lens is the tip of the image; the rest of the image simply stands on the axis below it.
